# ROOM — Product Vision & Knowledge Hub

ROOM is the shared reference for why NestScout exists, whom it serves and what a good service experience should feel like. It brings the founder’s documented direction into one place. Where a concept is still a proposal, it is named as a proposal; this document does not set new pricing, eligibility or operating policy.

## Vision

A home-service need should be easy to explain, understand and resolve with confidence.

NestScout begins with apartment residents in Ho Chi Minh City and aims to make the path from “something at home needs attention” to a clear, trusted service agreement feel straightforward.

## Mission

Help residents understand what kind of help they need, find suitable service professionals and agree on clear scope and transparent terms before work begins.

## Product philosophy

NestScout starts with the person’s situation, not a rigid service label. The product should help people describe the issue in their own words, then use service categories and expert context to make the next decision easier.

Clarity is part of the service itself. A Customer should be able to understand what is proposed, what a price covers, what remains uncertain and who needs to act next. Workers should receive enough context to respond responsibly and keep the Customer informed as work progresses.

Kael supports this exchange. It can help interpret a request, ask useful follow-up questions and surface grounded information. It does not become the source of truth for identity, availability, prices, consent or completed work. People decide; the system records and enforces the agreed action.

## Core values

These values express the founder’s documented product direction in concise terms. They are product principles, not new commercial rules.

| Value | What it means in the experience |
| --- | --- |
| **Clarity** | Make the request, proposed work, price basis and next step understandable. |
| **Trust through evidence** | Use real service records and attributable information; show when evidence is missing. |
| **Agency** | Keep choices, consent and consequential confirmations with the people affected. |
| **Respect on both sides** | Give Customers and Workers clear context, fair notice of changes and a record of decisions. |
| **Responsible assistance** | Let Kael explain uncertainty and support decisions without inventing facts or acting as an authority. |
| **Accountable follow-through** | Preserve the agreed scope, progress updates and completion evidence so that questions can be handled with context. |

## Positioning

NestScout is a guided home-services experience for apartment residents in Ho Chi Minh City. It connects a resident’s everyday description of a problem with service professionals and clear working agreements, with Kael helping people understand the request along the way.

The distinction is the whole journey: understand the need, make the options legible, and help both sides agree on what will happen.

## The Customer–Worker–Kael ecosystem

| Participant | Role in the service |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer** | Explains the need, reviews proposed scope and price, chooses whether to proceed, and approves changes that affect the agreement. |
| **Worker** | Shares service information, responds to an appropriate request, can propose scope changes where supported, communicates job updates and records submitted work. |
| **Kael Service Operator** | Helps interpret and clarify requests, retrieves grounded context, explains what is known or uncertain, and prepares supported actions for a person to review. |
| **Human service support** | Human judgment is part of the intended experience when a case needs it. The detailed operator workflow and interface are not defined here. |

A Customer or Worker mode describes the experience being used; it does not itself grant account permissions or establish a Worker’s professional qualification.

## Service groups

NestScout’s service catalog is organized around six everyday home needs:

1. Home cleaning
2. Electrical repair
3. Plumbing repair
4. Air-conditioning
5. Sofa, mattress, curtain and carpet care
6. Minor repair and installation

These groups help people orient themselves. They do not imply that every service or professional is available in every location or at every time.

## The user journey

1. **Start with the situation.** A Customer describes the issue in their own words and adds relevant details.
2. **Clarify what matters.** Kael or the service flow asks focused questions and makes missing information visible.
3. **Understand the options.** The Customer can review the kind of service, proposed scope, available evidence and any estimate with its basis and limits.
4. **Make a clear request.** The Customer confirms the request and can see what is, and is not, agreed.
5. **Review clear terms.** A quote, when available, states its proposed scope and price basis so the Customer can decide whether to accept it.
6. **Coordinate with a Worker.** A Worker invitation and response are separate from quote acceptance; an accepted quote alone does not confirm an assignment.
7. **Close with a record.** Work, decisions and completion evidence remain understandable, with an intended human support path for cases that need judgment.

An estimate is not an accepted quote. A proposed Worker, schedule or price remains a proposal until the relevant people confirm it. Accepting a quote does not assign a Worker; the invitation and response are separate. The current repository uses fixture quotes and offers and does not establish a commercial pricing or matching policy.

## Experience principles

- **Use everyday language.** Let people begin with what they observe; use service categories to guide, not constrain, the description.
- **Show the basis.** Explain what an estimate or quote covers and identify exclusions or uncertainty when known.
- **Make the next step visible.** Show who is expected to respond and what decision is waiting.
- **Keep consequential choices explicit.** A suggested action is not consent. People should confirm material service actions and changes.
- **Protect context.** Share only the information needed for the current service step, and give Workers enough detail to decide whether to respond.
- **Keep AI sharing understandable.** Explain when conversation information is shared for AI assistance and keep that choice separate and revocable.
- **Design for the local setting.** Use familiar Vietnamese service language, local context and clear VND amounts where price information is shown.
- **Leave room for human judgment.** Surface cases that cannot be responsibly resolved from the available information.

## How the application operates

NestScout is designed as one mobile experience for Customers and Workers, with discovery available before sign-in. An account may use more than one role. The chosen mode changes the screens and language of the interaction; the backend decides which data and actions the account is permitted to use.

Customer and Worker activity should be tied to a shared service record: the request, proposed terms, decisions, job progress and submitted evidence. This gives both sides a common account of what was agreed. Kael works alongside that record, using confirmed service state as the authority for business outcomes.

The intended service model keeps the Customer involved in decisions that change scope or price. Workers remain responsible for their proposals and work updates. Human operators provide judgment where automated assistance is insufficient; the operating model for that support remains open.

## Long-term direction

The first focus is apartment residents in Ho Chi Minh City. The longer-term ambition is to serve more of Vietnam and potentially parts of Asia, with broader reach depending on earned trust and consistent service quality.

A platform fee on successfully completed work has been discussed as a proposed business model. It is not a settled pricing policy, and this hub does not set fees, guarantees, Worker qualification rules or service availability.
